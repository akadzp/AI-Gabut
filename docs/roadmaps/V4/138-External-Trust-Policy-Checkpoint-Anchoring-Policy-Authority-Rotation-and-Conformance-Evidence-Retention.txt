Batch 138 — External Trust-Policy Checkpoint Anchoring, Policy-Authority Rotation and Revocation, and Conformance Evidence Retention

Objective
Protect provider-identity trust policies with an independent external checkpoint, govern the lifecycle of policy-authority keys, and retain provider-identity baseline conformance reports as auditable evidence.

Scope
1. Anchor per-owner drift-event, recovery-attestation, and policy-authority policy checkpoints through a dedicated external trust domain.
2. Enforce CAS, monotonic anchor sequence, digest continuity, and read-after-write verification.
3. Expose explicit enrollment for legacy local policies without claiming pre-enrollment history; reject automatic re-enrollment after a known anchor loss.
4. Rotate and revoke policy-authority keys using digest-bound challenges, authorization signatures, and replacement-key proof of possession.
5. Replay and verify historical authority transitions, including rejection of signatures created after retirement or compromise cutoff.
6. Automatically retain provider-identity baseline conformance reports in the bounded evidence registry and chain; audit binding and expiry/tombstone state.
7. Add authenticated APIs and cross-domain provider-independence checks.

Validation
- Focused regression tests: 6/6 PASS.
- Cumulative test suite: 956/956 PASS.
- Syntax, import resolution, archive integrity, and extracted-source tests: PASS.
- No GitHub commit.

Known boundaries
- External anchor durability and independent administration are properties of the deployed provider, not established by application code alone.
- Policy-authority key replacement must be preconfigured in the trusted public-key registry.
- Revoking the active authority key is blocked until a replacement is active; compromised-root recovery remains an out-of-band procedure.
- Tombstones preserve digests but cannot reconstruct expired payloads.
