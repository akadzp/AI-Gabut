Batch 113 — Ledger Gap Recovery and Tamper-Evidence Escalation Policy

Scope
- Recover only appendable, evidence-backed tail gaps in the Batch 112 mutation outcome ledger.
- Persist an owner-scoped escalation/quarantine policy outside the ledger so a broken hash chain can still be reported without rewriting the suspect chain.
- Enforce quarantine at the trusted mutation boundary and require an explicit acknowledgement after a complete, clean audit.

Implementation
- Added deterministic owner-scoped ledger escalation state with its own canonical SHA-256 digest, bounded incident history, severity (`high`/`critical`), reason codes, and active mutation-block flag.
- Added explicit gap recovery from immutable outcome evidence and approval resolution history. Reconstructed events are marked with a constrained recovery method and use deterministic event identities, so repeated recovery does not duplicate entries.
- Recovery refuses to rewrite a broken hash chain, reconstruct missing events that would need insertion into the middle of an existing chain, resolve conflicting outcomes, or act on incomplete/truncated audit results.
- Added mutation-boundary enforcement. A ledger gap or integrity finding is persisted as an escalation and prevents an approved mutation callback from running until the ledger audit is clean and a human explicitly acknowledges the active incident.
- Added explicit escalation acknowledgement. It requires an exact active incident ID, a bounded reason, and a fresh complete audit with no findings. It cannot clear a still-unhealthy ledger.
- Added read-only escalation inspection and authenticated HTTP routes for inspection, gap recovery, and acknowledgement.
- The policy record is independently digested and fails closed if its own integrity check fails.

Operational boundaries
- Gap recovery is explicit, append-only, and limited to events reconstructable from verified evidence or approval resolution history. It never repairs a broken hash chain in place.
- A high/critical incident remains active after the ledger becomes clean; an explicit acknowledgement is required. Acknowledgement cannot proceed until a fresh full audit is clean.
- SHA-256 digests are tamper-evident, not signatures. An actor able to rewrite all storage and recompute digests can forge a new history; stronger protection requires external signing or write-once storage.
- Atomic updates protect processes sharing the filesystem-backed storage root, not independent hosts.
- HTTP listener startup is not claimed as boot-tested in this environment because Express is not installed in the validation runtime.
